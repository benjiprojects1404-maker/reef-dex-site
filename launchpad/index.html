How a sale works

One contract per sale. No owner, no admin, and no way for anyone to take the BDAG.

  1. The project deposits first. Every token buyers will claim, plus the tokens for the pools, plus a 1,000 REEF bond. Buying stays switched off until it's all in.
  2. Buyers pay BDAG at a fixed price during the sale window, up to a per-wallet cap. They receive nothing yet.
  3. Anyone launches the pool once the sale ends (or hits its hard cap) above its soft cap. 2% goes to Reef's treasury. 80% of the rest opens a TOKEN/BDAG pool; 20% buys REEF for a TOKEN/REEF pool, only when that's safe, otherwise it all goes to TOKEN/BDAG. Every token not owed to a buyer goes to the dead address.
  4. Buyers claim their tokens whenever they like. The LP stays locked in the sale contract for 12 months, then can only go to the named beneficiary.
  5. If the sale fails, nobody loses BDAG. Under the soft cap, or not launched within 7 days of closing: every buyer refunds in full, and the project gets its tokens back.

For buyers

What the contract guarantees, and what it can't.

  • BDAG can only go into the pools, to the 2% fee, or back to you as a refund
  • Every sale's terms are fixed at deployment and readable on-chain
  • The pools open at the advertised price and the LP is locked
  • Unsold tokens are burned, never kept by the team
  • The contracts are not independently audited
  • After launch the token's price can fall well below what you paid
  • Part of the liquidity is a TOKEN/REEF pool, which also moves with REEF

For projects

A launch your buyers can check for themselves.

  • Sale contract set up, verified and listed with its own sale page
  • Two pools at launch: TOKEN/BDAG, plus TOKEN/REEF when it's safe
  • You set the prices, caps and dates; Reef's standard terms cover the rest
  • Deposit the sale tokens and a 1,000 REEF bond before it opens
  • Hand out no tokens before launch; the bond is burned if the launch is blocked
  • A standard ERC-20 only, and no promises about price

Why "beta". The sale contract hasn't had an independent audit yet. It has been through extensive testing (random-action fuzzing of every way a sale can go, and a full browser test of the sale page), and the builder's own self-review and results are public, as is the source. Fees from the first launches go towards an independent review; the 5M BDAG cap comes off after that. Until then, only use BDAG you're fully prepared to lose.
